CONTRAST

1

What is Contrast?

Contrast means making elements noticeably different from each other. This helps guide the viewer's attention to what's most important.

Why Use Contrast?

  • Makes important information easy to spot
  • Creates visual hierarchy (shows what to read first)
  • Makes text readable and designs professional
  • Adds visual interest to your work

You can create contrast using:

Colour

Dark vs light, warm vs cool

Size

Large vs small text or images

Weight

Bold vs regular text

Shape

Different shapes stand out

ALIGNMENT

1

What is Alignment?

Alignment means lining up elements along a common edge or centre line. Nothing should be placed randomly on the page.

Why Use Alignment?

  • Creates a clean, organised appearance
  • Makes designs look professional and intentional
  • Helps guide the reader's eye through the content
  • Creates visual connections between elements

Common Types of Alignment:

Left Aligned
Text starts here
On the left edge

Most common for body text

Centre Aligned
Text in middle
Balanced look

Good for titles and headings

Right Aligned
Text ends here
On the right edge

Less common, creates tension

Alignment Patterns Create Different Effects

The type of alignment you choose creates different feelings and serves different purposes. Let's explore how each pattern works:

Left Alignment: Strong & Professional

Creates a strong vertical edge that's easy to follow. Best for body text and professional documents.

Annual Report 2024

Our company achieved record growth this year, with revenue increasing by 35% across all divisions.

Customer satisfaction scores reached an all-time high of 94%.

Total Revenue: $2.4 million

Effect: Professional, easy to read, creates a clear starting point for the eye. The left edge acts as an anchor.

Centre Alignment: Formal & Balanced

Creates symmetry and elegance. Best for titles, invitations, and short pieces of text.

You're Invited

Year 8 Formal

Friday, December 15th at 7:00 PM

School Hall

Effect: Elegant and formal, creates visual balance. Works well for special occasions but harder to read in large blocks.

Right Alignment: Tension & Attention

Creates visual tension and draws attention. Best used sparingly for contrast or special emphasis.

Special Offer

Limited time only

50% OFF

Ends this weekend →

Effect: Unexpected and attention-grabbing. Creates tension because we're used to reading left-to-right. Use for impact!

Justified Alignment: Formal & Structured

Both left and right edges are aligned, creating a neat block. Common in newspapers and books.

Climate Study Results

Scientists have discovered new evidence of climate patterns affecting regional weather systems. The study, conducted over five years, examined temperature variations across multiple locations.

Researchers found that consistent monitoring is essential for understanding long-term environmental changes and predicting future trends.

Effect: Very formal and structured with clean edges. Can create awkward spacing in narrow columns. Common in print but less so digitally.

Mixing Alignments Intentionally

You can combine different alignments, but do it with purpose:

Product Launch Event

Date: March 15, 2024

Time: 2:00 PM - 4:00 PM

Location: Innovation Centre

RSVP by March 1st →

Centre-aligned title creates focus, left-aligned details are easy to scan, right-aligned RSVP draws attention. Each choice is intentional!

Alignment Key Takeaways

  • Every element should align with something else on the page
  • Consistent alignment creates a professional, organised look
  • Random placement makes designs look messy and amateur
  • Use alignment to create visual connections between elements

REPETITION

1

What is Repetition?

Repetition means repeating visual elements throughout your design. This creates consistency and helps tie everything together.

Why Use Repetition?

  • Creates a unified, cohesive design
  • Helps users understand your organisation system
  • Makes designs look professional and intentional
  • Reinforces branding and identity

What Can You Repeat?

Colours

Use the same colour scheme throughout

Fonts

Stick to 2-3 font families

Shapes

Repeat shapes and styles

Spacing

Use consistent spacing patterns

PROXIMITY

1

What is Proximity?

Proximity means grouping related items together and separating unrelated items. Things that are close together are perceived as belonging together.

Why Use Proximity?

  • Organises information into logical groups
  • Reduces clutter and confusion
  • Makes content easier to scan and understand
  • Shows relationships between elements

The Gestalt Principle

Our brains automatically group things that are close together. Proximity uses this natural tendency to organise information visually.

Proximity in Real Life

Proximity is everywhere! Here are examples from different contexts:

Newspapers

Articles are grouped with their headlines and images. White space separates different stories, making it easy to see where one ends and another begins.

Supermarkets

Products are grouped by category with clear aisle markers. Related items (pasta and pasta sauce) are placed near each other so shoppers can find everything they need.

Websites

Navigation links are grouped in a menu. Related content appears in the same section. Contact details are clustered together in the footer.

Maps

Tourist attractions are marked close together in popular areas. Transport hubs group multiple services. Landmarks help people understand relationships between locations.

Forms

Personal details (name, address) are grouped together. Payment information is in its own section. Related fields stay close, with extra space between different topics.

Product Packaging

Brand name and logo are together at the top. Ingredients list stays separate. Nutritional information is clustered in its own panel for easy reference.

Social Media

Each post groups the author's photo, name, content, and engagement buttons together. Clear spacing separates different posts in your feed.

Restaurant Menus

Dishes are grouped by category (starters, mains, desserts). Each item's description and price stay together, with space between different menu items.

Proximity Key Takeaways

  • Group related items close together
  • Separate unrelated items with white space
  • Proximity reduces clutter and improves organisation
  • Our brains naturally perceive close items as connected

Now that you've explored all four principles, remember:
